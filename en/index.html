How to use

The first run in a new area takes a little longer while it downloads data. If something breaks, check log.txt in the result folder.

  1. 1Pick a site

    Find the site on the map, or search by address or parcel number. Click parcels or draw an area to set the site.

  2. 2Make the 3D model

    Set the range and 3D options and press build. Buildings, roads, terrain and water go into your open Rhino file, layer by layer.

  3. 3Make the 2D set

    The basemap (.ai), analysis maps and tables (CSV) are saved to the result folder. Other basemap scales are one click away.

  4. 4Check the drawings

    Open any drawing large right in the panel. Change diagram colors and styles and make them again.

Coverage and data sources

South Korea · nationwide

Uses public data from MOLIT, NGII and Seoul UPIS. Regional data packs are downloaded once from inside the panel.

Uses MLIT PLATEAU, GSI and OpenStreetMap. Regional data is downloaded when a model is generated.

FAQ

Leave a review

Is it really free?

Yes, and no account is needed. It runs on sponsorships.

Do I need Grasshopper or other software?

No. You only need Rhino 8, SketchUp 2022+ or Blender 4.2+, on Windows or macOS.

Where are the results saved?

The 3D model goes into the open document. Files are saved to ~/Gorani/result/site_date/: the model file, log.txt and the 2D/ folder.

Something went wrong. What should I do?

First check log.txt in the result folder. If that doesn't solve it, open an issue on GitHub Issues and attach log.txt.

[고라니] [Water deer]

The water deer (Hydropotes inermis) is a small deer native to Korea and China. Males have long tusks instead of antlers, and usually live near rivers, reed beds and wetlands.

Taxonomy 분류

The water deer belongs to the deer family and is the only living species in the genus Hydropotes. Its lack of antlers and prominent tusks distinguish it from most other deer and give it a separate genus.

Etymology 어원

The genus name Hydropotes refers to water, reflecting the animal's association with rivers and wetlands. The species name inermis means unarmed, referring to the absence of antlers.
